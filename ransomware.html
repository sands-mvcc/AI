Ransomware: Analysis, Mapping, and Defense

Learning Objectives

  • Analyze historical ransomware campaigns to identify Kill Chain phases.
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of security controls (MFA, Segmentation, Patching).
  • Differentiate between extraction-based, worm-style, and destructive ransomware.
  • Design basic incident response plans based on lessons learned.

Cross-Cutting Themes

  • Identity is the Perimeter: Stolen credentials (VPN, RDP) are the #1 entry point (Colonial, Atlanta).
  • Supply Chain Risk: Trusted software updates can be weaponized (NotPetya, Kaseya).
  • Resilience vs. Prevention: You cannot prevent 100% of attacks. Offline backups and segmentation limit the blast radius.
  • IT/OT Convergence: Attacks on IT systems often force OT (Operational Technology) shutdowns due to lack of segmentation.

Recommended Teaching Sequence

  1. Lecture (15m): Review the "Case Studies" tab. Highlight the "Mistakes" vs "Lessons".
  2. Activity 1 (15m): Students complete "Kill Chain Mapping". Discuss results.
  3. Activity 2 (15m): "What If" Sandbox. Demonstrate how Worms spread vs. Credential attacks.
  4. Activity 3 (20m): "Controls Lab" & "Scenario Response".
  5. Assessment (10m): Final Quiz.

Incident Response Scenarios

Scenario A: The Late Night Call

Context: You are the CISO of a regional hospital. At 2:00 AM, the helpdesk reports that files on the main EMR server are being renamed with a ".LOCKED" extension. The wallpaper on three nursing stations has changed to a ransom note.

Task: Outline your immediate first 3 steps and your communication strategy.


Scenario B: The Vendor Notice

Context: Your MSP (Managed Service Provider) emails you stating they have been compromised (similar to Kaseya). They have administrative access to all your endpoints for patching.

Task: How do you proceed to protect your internal network?

Instructor Resources & Answer Keys

Discussion Prompts

  • Ethics: Should paying ransoms be illegal? (Ref: Colonial Pipeline paid, Atlanta did not).
  • Policy: How do we validate the security of our supply chain (SolarWinds/Kaseya)?
  • Technical: Why did WannaCry stop? (Kill switch). Why was NotPetya unrecoverable? (It was a wiper).

Controls Mapping Key

  • WannaCry/EternalBlue: Patching (MS17-010) or Disable SMBv1.
  • Colonial/VPN: Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
  • NotPetya/Update: Network Segmentation (limit reach of supply chain tool).
  • Atlanta/RDP: VPN/Allow-listing or Offline Backups for recovery.
