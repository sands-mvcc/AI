Creating AI-Resistant Assignments

Welcome to this professional development workshop on designing assignments that promote genuine learning while addressing the challenges posed by generative AI tools.

Workshop Objectives

By the end of this workshop, you will be able to:

  • Understand the principles behind AI-resistant assignment design
  • Apply multiple strategies to modify existing assignments
  • Create authentic assessments that promote deeper learning
  • Develop a balanced approach to AI in your classroom

What Makes an Assignment "AI-Resistant"?

An AI-resistant assignment is not about creating barriers or "tricking" students. Instead, it's about designing assessments that:

  • Promote genuine learning and critical thinking skills
  • Require personal insight that AI cannot replicate
  • Focus on the learning process rather than just the final product
  • Connect to real-world applications and authentic contexts
  • Make AI collaboration transparent when appropriate

Important Note: The goal is not to make assignments "AI-proof" (which may be impossible), but rather to design assessments where using AI provides limited benefit or where AI use must be transparent and critically evaluated.

Why Create AI-Resistant Assignments?

The Challenge

Current generative AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and others can:

  • Generate sophisticated-sounding academic writing
  • Complete traditional essays, problem sets, and assignments
  • Produce code, create presentations, and solve complex problems
  • Operate quickly and accessibly for students

The Opportunity

Rather than viewing AI solely as a threat, this is an opportunity to:

  • Rethink assessment practices that may have become rote or formulaic
  • Focus on higher-order thinking skills that are essential in an AI-driven world
  • Create more authentic and engaging learning experiences
  • Prepare students to work effectively alongside AI tools
  • Emphasize uniquely human skills like creativity, emotional intelligence, and ethical reasoning

Reflection Exercise

Think about one assignment you currently use in your courses. Ask yourself:

  • Could ChatGPT complete this assignment and receive a passing grade?
  • What makes this assignment meaningful for student learning?
  • How could I modify it to emphasize uniquely human elements?

Understanding Student Motivation

Students may use AI for various reasons:

  • Assignments perceived as busywork or lacking relevance
  • Time management difficulties and stress
  • Fear of failure
  • Lack of understanding about academic integrity
  • Legitimate uses like proofreading or generating ideas (AI-as-tutor)

Understanding these motivations helps us design better assignments that address the root causes of academic dishonesty while supporting authentic learning.
