What is Prompt Engineering?

Prompt engineering is the practice of crafting inputs (prompts) to AI systems that yield the most accurate, relevant, and useful outputs. Think of it as learning the "language" that helps AI understand exactly what you need.

🎯 Classroom Analogy

Imagine giving instructions to a substitute teacher who's highly capable but doesn't know your classroom routines. A vague instruction like "teach the students" won't work well. But specific guidance like "You're an experienced 5th-grade science teacher. Use hands-on demonstrations and relate concepts to everyday life" yields much better results. That's prompt engineering.

Why This Matters for Education

  • Student Preparation: Students will use AI throughout their careers; teaching them to use it effectively is essential
  • Enhanced Productivity: Educators can create lesson plans, assessments, and materials more efficiently
  • Critical Thinking: Understanding prompt engineering develops metacognitive skills about communication and problem decomposition
  • Ethical AI Use: Knowing how prompts work helps educators guide students toward responsible AI usage

Style 1 Role-Based Prompting (MIT Style)

📖 Definition

Role-Based prompting assigns a specific persona, profession, or perspective to the AI, leveraging specialized domain knowledge and appropriate communication styles. This technique, popularized at MIT and other leading institutions, helps the AI adopt the right "mindset" for your task.

🎭 Teaching Analogy

Think of an actor preparing for a role. Before performing, they research their character's background, profession, and motivations. Similarly, when you assign a role to AI, you're helping it "get into character" as an expert in that domain. A financial analyst thinks differently than a creative writer, and role-based prompting captures these distinctions.

🔑 Key Concepts

  • Persona Assignment: Explicitly state who the AI should "be" (e.g., "You are a high school biology teacher")
  • Expertise Level: Specify the depth of knowledge needed (novice, intermediate, expert)
  • Perspective Framing: Define the viewpoint from which to approach the task
  • Contextual Constraints: Include relevant situational factors (audience, purpose, format)

📋 How to Use It (Directions)

  1. Start with "You are...": Begin your prompt by explicitly assigning the role
  2. Add qualifiers: Include expertise level, years of experience, or specific credentials
  3. Define the context: Explain the situation or audience
  4. State the task: Clearly articulate what you need the AI to do

💡 Educational Examples

Example 1: Lesson Planning

"You are an experienced middle school math teacher with 15 years of classroom experience. Create a 45-minute lesson plan on introducing algebraic expressions to 7th graders who have never seen variables before. Include differentiation strategies for struggling learners."

Example 2: Assessment Creation

"You are a college-level psychology professor specializing in cognitive development. Design a rubric for evaluating student research papers on Piaget's stages of development. The rubric should assess understanding of theory, application of concepts, and critical analysis."

Example 3: Student Support

"You are a patient and encouraging reading specialist working with elementary students. Explain the difference between consonant blends and digraphs using simple language and everyday examples that a 2nd grader would understand."

🎓 Tips for Teaching This Style

✓ Start with familiar roles: Have students practice with roles they understand (teacher, scientist, historian)

✓ Compare outputs: Show how the same question gets different answers with different roles

✓ Discuss expertise: Use this to teach about how professionals think differently in their domains

✓ Connect to audience awareness: This reinforces writing to different audiences

⚠️ Common Pitfalls & Solutions

Mistake Why It's Problematic Better Approach
Too vague: "You are a teacher" Doesn't specify subject, level, or expertise "You are a high school chemistry teacher with AP experience"
No context for audience Output may not match student level Include: "explaining to students who struggle with math"
Contradictory roles "You are a beginner expert..." creates confusion Choose one clear expertise level

Style 2 Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting

📖 Definition

Chain-of-Thought prompting instructs the AI to break down complex problems into logical, sequential steps, making the reasoning process transparent and improving accuracy on multi-step tasks. This technique mirrors how expert problem-solvers think aloud.

🧩 Teaching Analogy

Imagine teaching a student to solve a word problem. Instead of jumping to the answer, you guide them: "First, identify what information you have. Next, determine what you need to find. Then, choose the appropriate operation..." CoT prompting teaches AI to follow this same disciplined approach, making errors easier to spot and reasoning clearer.

🔑 Key Concepts

  • Step-by-Step Reasoning: Breaking complex problems into manageable sub-tasks
  • Explicit Thinking: Making the reasoning process visible and traceable
  • Logical Sequencing: Following a clear progression from problem to solution
  • Error Detection: Easier to identify where reasoning goes wrong
  • Teaching Tool: Models good problem-solving strategies for students

📋 How to Use It (Directions)

  1. Add the instruction: Include phrases like "think step-by-step" or "break this down into steps"
  2. Be specific about steps: If needed, outline the logical sequence you want
  3. Request intermediate work: Ask the AI to show its reasoning at each stage
  4. Verify logic: Check that each step follows from the previous

💡 Educational Examples

Example 1: Math Problem Solving

"Solve this problem step-by-step: A classroom has 24 students. If 1/3 are absent and 2 more leave early, how many remain? Show your thinking at each step."

Why it works: The AI will calculate 1/3 of 24, subtract that, then subtract 2 more, showing each calculation.

Example 2: Scientific Reasoning

"A student claims plants can grow without sunlight if given extra water. Evaluate this claim step-by-step: (1) Explain what plants need sunlight for, (2) Identify what water provides, (3) Determine if one can replace the other, (4) State your conclusion with evidence."

Example 3: Writing Process

"Help a student develop a thesis statement for an essay on climate change. Think step-by-step: First, identify the specific aspect of climate change they want to focus on. Second, determine their position on that aspect. Third, craft a clear, arguable statement. Fourth, check that it's specific enough for a 5-page essay."

🎓 Tips for Teaching This Style

✓ Model metacognition: Show students how experts "think about thinking"

✓ Compare with/without CoT: Demonstrate how step-by-step improves accuracy

✓ Use for complex tasks: Math, science experiments, multi-paragraph writing

✓ Teach debugging: When AI (or students) get wrong answers, trace through steps to find errors

✓ Build gradually: Start with 2-3 steps, increase complexity over time

🎯 When to Use Chain-of-Thought

Best For Not Ideal For
Math word problems requiring multiple operations Simple factual questions ("What is the capital of France?")
Scientific reasoning and hypothesis evaluation Creative writing without specific logical constraints
Multi-step instructions or procedures Quick definitions or vocabulary explanations
Logical argument construction Open-ended brainstorming

🎓 Teaching Progression: Building Student Mastery

Week 1-2: Foundations

  • Start with Zero-Shot (clarity in instructions)
  • Introduce Role-Based (perspective and audience awareness)

Week 3-4: Structure & Patterns

  • Add Instructional Prompting (precise specifications)
  • Introduce Few-Shot (learning from examples)

Week 5-6: Reasoning & Thinking

  • Teach Chain-of-Thought (step-by-step reasoning)
  • Introduce Generated Knowledge (activating prior knowledge)

Week 7-8: Advanced Techniques

  • Explore Self-Consistency (verification methods)
  • Introduce Style Matching (audience adaptation)

Week 9-10: Expert Level

  • Teach ReAct (combining reasoning with action)
  • Introduce Meta-Prompting (optimization and templates)

⚡ Quick Decision Flowchart

START: What's your primary goal?

→ Multi-step problem? Use Chain-of-Thought

→ Specific format required? Use Few-Shot or Instructional

→ Match existing style/tone? Use Style Matching

→ Need current information? Use ReAct

→ Complex analysis needed? Use Generated Knowledge

→ Accuracy is critical? Use Self-Consistency
