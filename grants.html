NSF AI Grant Opportunities

Prepared for Central Wyoming College Executive Team
Presentation Date: January 2026
Focus: AI & Cybersecurity

Executive Overview

Our research into the National Science Foundation (NSF) landscape identifies a significant opportunity for Central Wyoming College to secure funding for Artificial Intelligence (AI) program development.

Key Finding

The ATE (Advanced Technological Education) program is our strongest entry point. It is congressionally mandated to support technician education at 2-year colleges. However, new programs like ExpandAI offer unique "capacity building" funds specifically for institutions outside the major research hubs.

Why Now?

  • Workforce Demand: Regional need for AI-literate technicians is growing.
  • NSF Mandate: The NSF is actively seeking to fund rural and community college AI initiatives to democratize access to technology.
  • Faculty Readiness: Our current expertise in Cybersecurity provides the perfect foundation for an AI expansion.
Top Priority

Advanced Technological Education (ATE)

Purpose: Focuses on the education of technicians for high-technology fields. This is the gold standard for community college funding.

Grant Size (Project)

Up to $650,000

Duration

3 Years

Deadline

Early October

Strategy for CWC

We should propose a "New-to-ATE" project or a standard Project grant to develop an "AI Technician Certificate."

  • Curriculum: Develop 3-4 new courses in AI fundamentals and ML Operations.
  • Equipment: Upgrade labs with GPU-capable workstations.
  • Industry: Partner with local energy or tech sectors to validate the skills matrix.
Capacity Building

ExpandAI

Purpose: Designed to significantly broaden participation in AI research and education. It targets institutions that have not yet fully benefited from NSF AI investments.

Concept Grants

$200,000

Partnerships

Up to $1.2M

Deadline

Rolling / Seasonal

Why This Fits CWC

Unlike ATE (which is workforce-only), ExpandAI can help us build the infrastructure and faculty expertise needed to teach high-level AI concepts. It encourages partnerships with established AI Institutes at larger universities.

Pedagogy & Transfer

IUSE: Innovation in Two-Year College STEM Education (ITYC)

Purpose: A specific track dedicated solely to two-year colleges to improve student success, retention, and transfer rates in STEM fields.

Funding Limit

Up to $500,000

Focus Area

Student Success

Deadline

May (Annual)

Application

We could utilize this to fund a "Bridge Program" that supports students moving from our 2-year AI program to a 4-year Computer Science degree.

Recommended Strategic Roadmap

To maximize our chances of success, I recommend the following phased approach:


Phase 1: Secure the Foundation (Year 1)
Target: NSF ATE Program

Develop an "AI Technician" certificate. Establish an Industry Advisory Board to validate local workforce needs. This is our "low-hanging fruit."

Phase 2: Build Capacity (Year 1-2)
Target: ExpandAI

While the ATE grant funds the curriculum, use ExpandAI to fund faculty professional development and partnership building with a major university AI Institute.

Phase 3: Support Students (Year 3)
Target: S-STEM

Once the program is established, apply for S-STEM to provide scholarships that attract low-income, high-talent students to the new AI major.
